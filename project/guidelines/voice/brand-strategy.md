# Luma — Brand strategy & messaging

**Working version 1.0 · November 2026.** This is the source of truth for every word Luma publishes: website, product listings, packaging, email, social, ads, founder content, customer service and UI microcopy. It encodes the founder's strategy brief. Figures and conditional claims are **provisional** until the launch-readiness checklist (§9) is complete.

---

## 1. The idea

| | |
|---|---|
| **Brand idea** | **Light, reimagined.** A different approach to the light coming from the screen you use every day. |
| **Product headline** | **A different kind of screen protector.** |
| **Brand promise** | **Rethink what's between you and your screen.** |
| **Category** | Light-converting screen protection |
| **Personality** | Curious. Precise. Confident. Considered. |
| **Commercial strategy** | Discover through curiosity. Convert through clarity and evidence. |
| **Long-term ambition** | A trusted consumer-technology brand focused on how people interact with light. |

**Positioning.** Luma creates thoughtfully engineered screen protection for people who care about the technology they use every day. Beginning with LC–01, Luma brings light-conversion technology into an everyday phone accessory, combining screen protection with a different approach to managing emitted light.

**What Luma is:** a science-led consumer technology brand.
**What Luma is not:** a sleep supplement disguised as a phone accessory, or another blue-light-blocking protector.

### The one rule
**Light technology is the central promise.** Sleep, evening routines and wellness are *contexts* we can talk about. They are never *outcomes* we promise. Separate what attracts people to the product from what the product has been shown to do.

### The reaction we want
> "I didn't know a screen protector could do that. That's interesting. I want to understand it."
…and then enough evidence and clarity to buy with confidence.

### Proposition, three layers
- **Functional:** protects your phone screen while changing some of the light passing through it.
- **Emotional:** the feeling of having made a more considered choice about everyday technology.
- **Identity:** belonging to the people who like to understand, and improve, the tools they use.

## 2. Who we're talking to

One brand, two audiences. Every page should answer both questions.

| | Audience A — The intentional technology buyer | Audience B — The wellness optimiser |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Primary **positioning** audience | Primary **content-discovery** audience |
| Who | Buys good technology, notices design, wants to know how things work | Into sleep tracking, recovery, routines, wearables, light exposure |
| Attracted by | A clever feature, credible engineering, a premium experience | Education about screens and evening habits |
| Asks | *Is this clever, well-engineered and worth paying more for?* | *Does this fit the way I think about my daily habits?* |
| We answer with | Mechanism, materials, measurements, finish | Clear light education, considered routines — **no sleep promises** |

Test the two audiences against each other. Don't assume the wellness audience converts better.

## 3. Messaging architecture

Four levels. Use them in this order on any surface with room for a story; use the highest level that fits on surfaces without room.

| Level | Line | Job | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Brand | **Light, reimagined.** | The recognisable idea. Broad enough for future products. | Brand moments, packaging lid, sign-offs, footer, ticker |
| 2 · Product | **A different kind of screen protector.** | The curiosity hook. Something unusual in a familiar category. | Homepage hero, PDP, ads, listing hero |
| 3 · Explanation | **Not just protection. Light conversion.** | The functional distinction. *Conditional* — mechanism must be proven on the final sample. | "The difference" sections, explainers, carousels |
| 4 · Proof | **Measured, not assumed.** | Optical results, methods, materials, limitations. | Results sections, Science page, proof posts |

**Do not lead with 17.17%.** On its own it isn't exciting, and competitors advertise bigger numbers. The mechanism is the distinctive story; the numbers are the evidence underneath it.

### Approved supporting lines
- Most screen protectors protect the glass. LC–01 is designed to do something more.
- Engineered to convert a portion of blue light into longer wavelengths. *(conditional)*
- Blocking light and converting light aren't the same thing.
- Your screen protector could do more than protect your screen.
- Small changes. Different light.
- Technology for better screen time. *(mood line only — never next to sleep, eye or health language)*

### Message territories to test
| Territory | Lead line | Support |
|---|---|---|
| **Technology** *(develop first)* | A different kind of screen protector. | Engineered to change more than what happens when you drop your phone. |
| **Light** | Light, reimagined. | A different approach to the light from the screen you use every day. |
| **Science** | Measured, not assumed. | What we tested, how, and what it doesn't mean. |

## 4. Tone of voice

Intelligent without being clinical or cold.

### Five principles
| Principle | Means | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Curious** | Make people interested in something they've never considered | Open with a question or a surprising fact | Open with a discount or a superlative |
| **Precise** | Explain what happens without exaggerating | "a portion of blue light" · "on a test sample" | "eliminates blue light" · "proven" |
| **Confident** | Clear statements instead of hype | Short declaratives that end in a full stop | Exclamation marks, "revolutionary", "game-changing" |
| **Accessible** | Science anyone can understand | Gloss every technical term the first time | Unexplained jargon ("QD-film", "nm peak shift") |
| **Considered** | Every word intentional | One idea per sentence; cut the second adjective | Filler, stacked benefits, urgency tricks |

### Say this, not that
| Not Luma | Luma |
|---|---|
| Revolutionary quantum technology that transforms your phone into a powerful wellness device. | Most screen protectors protect the glass. LC–01 is designed to do something more. |
| Block harmful blue light and sleep better tonight! | LC–01 converts a portion of blue light into longer wavelengths. Here's how we measured it. |
| Clinically proven 17% blue light reduction. | 17.17% blue-light reduction, reported on a TÜV test sample. |
| 28.72% of blue light turned into healing red light. | 28.72% conversion efficiency — a manufacturer-defined test metric. It isn't the share of all screen blue light that's converted. |
| Red-light therapy from your phone. | *(Never. No equivalent.)* |
| Protect your eyes from screen damage. | *(Never. No equivalent.)* |
| Crystal-clear HD display guaranteed. | Specified at around 91% light transmission. |

### Two voices
| | **Brand voice** | **Founder voice** |
|---|---|---|
| Feels | Calm, editorial, refined | Human, curious, occasionally playful |
| Used on | Website, packaging, product descriptions, listings, education pages, email, paid ads | TikTok/Reels/Shorts, founder posts, launch-list emails signed by the founder, podcasts |
| Can | State, explain, show evidence | Ask questions, challenge assumptions, document the process, admit uncertainty |
| Example | "Light, reimagined." | "I found a manufacturer claiming their screen protector converts blue light into red. So I wanted to see if it actually works." |
| Same in both | **The claims rules.** A founder can be informal; they can't be less accurate. |

### Mechanics
- **Person:** "we" for Luma, "you/your" for the reader — sparingly. Prefer describing the light and the product over describing the customer.
- **Casing:** sentence case everywhere — headlines, buttons, nav, labels. Uppercase buttons and overlines are a *type treatment*; write the source text in sentence case ("Shop LC–01").
- **Headlines** are short declaratives with a full stop. Two-beat constructions are signature: "Small changes. Different light." · "Not just protection. Light conversion."
- **Product name:** "Luma LC–01" first mention, then "LC–01". En dash, never hyphen. Descriptor: "Quantum-Dot Screen Protector" (title case, as a product name).
- **Numbers:** digits, units, and a qualifier — always. "≈ 91%" with a thin space. "0.33 mm" with a space.
- **Punctuation:** no exclamation marks. Em dashes sparingly. British spelling (colour, licence, optimise).
- **No emoji** in brand voice. Founder voice may use them natively on personal channels, never in brand-owned assets.
- **Jargon:** "quantum dots" is fine with a gloss — "particles a few nanometres across that change the colour of light".

### Vocabulary
**Use:** light, wavelengths, convert, a portion of, designed to, engineered to, measured, test sample, specification, considered, everyday, clarity, applicator, straight fit.
**Avoid:** harmful, toxic, damage, protect your eyes, sleep better, melatonin, circadian, therapy, healing, detox, clinically proven, guaranteed, eliminates, blocks 100%, revolutionary, game-changing, miracle, hack, "Hurry", "Last chance".

## 5. Claims register

**Statuses.** **Conditional** — may be used in designs and drafts now, *always with the qualifier*; must be confirmed on the production sample before anything goes live. **Avoid** — never, in any voice, on any channel.

| Claim | Status | Required wording / why |
|---|---|---|
| "Engineered to convert a portion of blue light into longer wavelengths" | Conditional | Validate on the exact production sample. Always "a portion". |
| "17.17% blue-light reduction" | Conditional | Always with "reported TÜV test sample"; publish range and method once received. |
| "Maintains display clarity" | Conditional | Verify transmission and real-world experience. Until then: "designed for clarity". |
| "Protects your screen" | Conditional | Verify material and protection performance. |
| "Improves sleep quality" | **Avoid** | No product-specific human evidence. |
| "Red-light therapy from your phone" | **Avoid** | Optical conversion is not therapeutic-dose evidence. |
| "Protects your eyes from damage" | **Avoid** | No relevant clinical evidence. |
| Any melatonin, circadian, eye-strain or skin claim | **Avoid** | Same reason. |
| "Clinically proven" / "doctor recommended" | **Avoid** | No clinical studies. |
| "The first / only light-converting protector" | **Avoid** | Competitors already sell light-converting protectors. |

### Figures register
| Figure | Label | Qualifier (always shown) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **17.17%** | Blue-light reduction | Reported TÜV test sample | Provisional — full report and SKU match pending |
| **28.72%** | Conversion efficiency | Manufacturer-defined test metric | Provisional — explain what it means wherever shown |
| **≈ 91%** | Light transmission | Product specification | Unverified — supplier docs also cite ~95% |
| **9H** | Surface hardness | Product specification | Requires final-product confirmation |
| **0.33 mm** | Glass thickness | Product specification | Material (tempered glass vs PET vs layered) to be confirmed |

**Explaining 28.72%.** "28.72% conversion efficiency is a manufacturer-defined measurement taken under specific test conditions. It doesn't mean 28.72% of all the blue light from your screen becomes red light." Use this, or a shorter version, whenever the number appears.

**Disclaimer** — on every surface showing figures: *"LC–01 is a screen protector, not a medical device. Figures describe a measured test sample and are not health claims."*

**Context for the team** (not marketing copy): a 2023 Cochrane review (17 trials, 619 participants) found little or no evidence that blue-light-filtering *glasses* reduce short-term eyestrain, with sleep outcomes uncertain. The UK ASA has upheld a ruling against a blue-light-glasses brand for unsubstantiated melatonin and sleep claims. Our advantage is being the brand that says precisely what the product does — and what it doesn't.

## 6. Touchpoint playbook

### Website — homepage sequence
1. **Hero — curiosity.** "A different kind of screen protector." Premium product image. CTA "Explore LC–01".
2. **The difference — mechanism.** "Not just protection. Light conversion." Conventional protection vs what LC–01 is designed to do.
3. **The science — evidence.** "Measured, not assumed." Optical results, test conditions, limitations.
4. **The product — remove friction.** Compatibility, installation, materials, clarity, delivery and returns.
5. **The brand — why Luma exists.** "Rethink what's between you and your screen."
6. **Conversion — next step.** Choose your iPhone model, or join the launch list.

**Science page:** the full method and limitations — keep technical depth here, not on the homepage. **Understanding light** (planned): education, not selling — no product CTA above the fold.

### Product page / listings
Lead with Level 2, explain with Level 3, prove with Level 4. Specs table carries every qualifier. FAQ answers "Will it help me sleep?" honestly: *"We don't make sleep or eye-health claims. LC–01 changes some of the light passing through it, and we publish exactly what we measured."*

### Packaging
Lid: logo + "Light, reimagined." Front: "Luma LC–01 · Quantum-Dot Screen Protector". Back: one-paragraph mechanism, figures with qualifiers, install steps, disclaimer. No sleep or health language anywhere on pack.

### Email
Subject lines are curious, not urgent: "What 28.72% actually means" · "Blocking light vs converting it" · "We tested the claims. Here's what we found." Never "Last chance" or countdown timers.

### Social & ads — hooks to test
1. **Discovery** *(founder video — strongest)*: "I found a screen protector that claims to convert blue light into red light. Here's what I found when I looked into it."
2. **Science** *(carousel)*: "Blocking light and converting light aren't the same thing."
3. **Product** *(demo)*: "Your screen protector could do more than protect your screen."
4. **Transparency** *(breakdown)*: "The manufacturer says 28.72% conversion efficiency. But what does that number actually mean?"
5. **Lifestyle** *(editorial)*: "We've become incredibly intentional about our routines. What about the technology we use every day?"

### Content pillars — first experiment (12 short videos)
- 4 × **The surprising technology** — how light conversion works.
- 4 × **Testing the claims** — scrutinising manufacturer figures, designing LC–01.
- 4 × **Everyday light** — technology choices and light education.

Measure qualified site visits, email sign-ups and purchase intent — not views. A viral sleep video is only useful if it brings customers who understand what they're buying.

### UI microcopy
Literal and brief. "Add to bag" · "Checkout" · "Choose your model" · "Your bag is empty." · "Enter a valid email address." · "You're on the list." · "Something went wrong. Try again."

### Customer service
Same voice, warmer. Answer the question in the first sentence. If a customer asks about sleep or eyes, give the honest answer from the FAQ — don't speculate.

## 7. Differentiation

Competitors (e.g. BodyGuardz) already market red-light-converting protectors with broader skin and red-light claims. **Luma never claims to have invented light conversion.** We differentiate on execution:

| Dimension | Luma |
|---|---|
| Product | Purpose-built, premium screen protection |
| Story | Understand and rethink everyday light |
| Design | Science-led consumer-technology aesthetic |
| Trust | Product-specific measurements and transparent limitations |
| Experience | Clarity, installation and packaging — once validated |
| Community | Educational content and founder-led development |

The distinction isn't quantum dots in a screen protector. It's making an unfamiliar technology understandable, desirable and trustworthy.

---

## 8. Copy QA — before anything ships
1. Does it lead with the mechanism or curiosity, not a number?
2. Does every figure carry its qualifier from the figures register?
3. Is every claim in the register — and not marked Avoid?
4. Could anyone read a sleep, eye, skin or therapy benefit into it?
5. Is the disclaimer present wherever figures appear?
6. Sentence case, no exclamation marks, no emoji (brand voice), LC–01 with an en dash?
7. Could a smart 14-year-old explain it back to you?

## 9. Launch readiness — gates for every Conditional claim
- [ ] Confirm LC–01 construction: tempered glass, PET or layered.
- [ ] Obtain the complete TÜV report — wavelengths, methods, sample ID.
- [ ] Verify the report applies to the exact production SKU.
- [ ] Commission independent optical testing if supplier documentation is insufficient.
- [ ] Test display clarity, colour accuracy, touch response and installation.
- [ ] Validate commercial claims against UK advertising requirements (CAP/ASA).
- [ ] Interview 5–10 potential buyers on value proposition and price.
- [ ] Test curiosity-led content and a purchase-intent landing page.

When an item is complete, update the figures register above first, then every surface that shows that figure.
